Research Philosophy

研究理念

As an assistant professor in the geotechnical engineering group at NCKU, I am committed to advancing our ability to understand and predict how soils behave under complex, real-world conditions — from extreme rainfall and climate change to seismic loading.

My approach combines three pillars: advanced laboratory testing to capture the true mechanical behaviour of soils, innovative field monitoring to bridge the gap between lab and reality, and numerical modelling to integrate these insights into predictive tools for engineering practice.

I believe that improving geotechnical resilience starts from the fundamentals — understanding what the soil is truly doing — and extends through data-driven methods, constitutive modelling, and digital twin technologies to enable smarter, more proactive infrastructure management.
